Response 1 (Behavioral)
Question: "Tell me about a time you performed an access review and found a discrepancy."

Situation: In the identity and access management lab of my Microsoft Cloud for Healthcare program, I was given a sandbox tenant for a simulated outpatient clinic with more than 20 user accounts that could reach patient records.

Task: My job was to review every user account, group membership, and role-based permission in Microsoft Entra ID and confirm that each person's access matched their job role under the HIPAA minimum-necessary standard.

Action: I exported the user and group lists from Entra ID, compared each account against the clinic's role matrix, and found 3 over-provisioned accounts; the most serious was a front-desk scheduler still in a nursing group that gave read access to patient charts (PHI). I documented the finding, moved the scheduler into the correct scheduling-only group, and checked the Entra audit and sign-in logs to confirm no patient records had been opened through that access.

Result: All 3 accounts were brought back to least privilege without breaking anyone's daily work, and I added a quarterly group-membership review to the lab's access checklist so role changes would not leave extra PHI access behind.


Response 2 (Scenario-based)
Question: "What would you do if a newly onboarded clinical coordinator cannot access the scheduling system on their first day?"

Situation: I handled almost this exact ticket in my program's help-desk lab, where a newly created clinical coordinator account in a simulated clinic could not sign in to the scheduling application on day one.

Task: I needed to restore access quickly so patient appointments were not delayed, while giving the coordinator only the permissions their role required.

Action: I followed a structured diagnostic process: I confirmed the account was enabled and licensed in Microsoft Entra ID, then checked the Entra sign-in logs, which showed a Conditional Access block because multifactor authentication was not yet registered, and found the account was also missing from the scheduling app's security group. I walked the coordinator through MFA registration, added them to the scheduling-coordinator group rather than a broader clinical group, and watched the sign-in logs while they tried again.

Result: The coordinator was scheduling patients within the same session with least-privilege access, and I documented both root causes in the ticket and recommended adding MFA registration and group assignment to the new-hire onboarding checklist so future clinical staff would not hit the same block.
